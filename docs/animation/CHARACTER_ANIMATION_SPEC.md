# Character animation production specification

**Status: original production specification, updated baseline.** Five original skinned character GLBs and keyframed clips now exist and are integrated in source: astronaut, human NPC, PIP, Scout and optional Cometa. Eight earlier GLBs are retained, for 13 local models total. `content-design/animation-manifest.json` records the generated character assets and `public/assets/manifest.json` their provenance. The text below retains design targets and acceptance criteria; it does not assert that every transition or visual goal has passed.

## Baseline and ownership

`src/actors/movement.ts` and the actor/controller code govern movement; the simulation owns time and resources. Animation is presentation and cannot grant resources, perform repairs or decide mission success. Cometa is optional, with follow, wait and recall behavior; her state is included in the current simulation/save implementation. Follow behavior is not proof of collision-safe general navigation.

The initial Wave 3 CPU check recorded 64 unit tests across eight files. Asset and NullEngine checks do not prove visual acceptance. Software-rendered Chromium runs include a failed attempt and a slow aborted attempt; the later run was in progress at this update. Consult `../../TEST_REPORT.md` and `../demo/E2E_REPORT.json` for actual results. Physical-GPU animation review, target-device profiling and a complete 20-transition stability test remain pending.

## Asset and rig handoff

The five character GLBs under `public/assets/models/characters/` are generated by `tools/models/build_characters.mjs`. Preserve source, manifest hashes, licensing and original designs. The following paragraphs describe the continuing rig and review contract.

Use meters, Y-up, grounded origin and a documented forward axis; verify glTF-to-Babylon conversion and actual world matrices on import rather than assuming a sign or applying a second correction. Record source and runtime forward/up axes, units, handedness, transform hierarchy, mesh/material bindings, inverse bind matrices, rest/bind pose, joint names/paths and actual bounds in the delivered manifest. Freeze unapplied transforms before binding; consistent rest pose, normalized skin weights and nonzero scales. The visual skeleton sits under a controller-owned wrapper; skin and attachment nodes belong to that skeleton. Name actual exported nodes after verification rather than assuming a proposed name survived export. Preserve helmet, suit, facial and material bindings. Check skinning in an independent GLB viewer and Babylon.

Human astronaut and NPC may share a compatible human rig only after hierarchy, bind pose, proportions and weights are verified; adapt poses for helmets and suits. PIP and Scout need their own mechanical body/head/eye/track or wheel controls where present, with constraints appropriate to the actual model; never retarget a humanoid skeleton onto robots. Cometa requires a separate quadruped skeleton: pelvis, restrained multi-joint spine, neck/head, left/right front and rear upper/lower limbs and paws, ears and tail/sleeve, with suit-compatible deformation. The rigid clear helmet follows the head without intersecting floppy ears throughout motion. Low pack and suit maintain clearance. Four paw contacts and support joints matter; human foot-only matching is insufficient.

## Rig-specific animation sets

The table describes intended semantics. Authored clip names and timing are recorded in the animation manifest; the presence of a clip does not establish visual acceptance or runtime use of every extended action. Each left/right turn is separately authored or explicitly validated as a mirrored asset; a mechanical `TurnLeftRight` is a directional family, not a single invented clip. In-place loops leave translation and yaw to the controller. Record actual frame rate, source frame bounds, duration, loop seam and marker times only after authoring. Unknown data is `null` in the manifest.

| Rig | Core clips and semantics | Extended clips |
|---|---|---|
| Astronaut | `Idle`; `WalkEarth`, `RunEarth` for academy ground; `WalkLunar`, `RunLunar` for lunar exterior; `TurnLeft`, `TurnRight` for heading changes; `Stop` for arrival; `Inspect`, `Repair` for visual interaction; `JumpTakeoff`, `Airborne`, `Land` depict the existing controller-owned jump. | `Talk` during an encounter; `PetCometa` when the optional buddy is safely positioned. |
| Human NPC | `Idle`, `Talk`, `Gesture`, `Point` for a stationary encounter. | `Walk`, `TurnLeft`, `TurnRight` only for NPCs that need movement. |
| PIP and Scout | `Idle`, `Travel`, `TurnLeft`, `TurnRight`, `Scan`, `Acknowledge`: mechanical travel, directional turning, instrument observation and a readable response. Animate body, head and eyes independently where available; track/wheel visual rotation follows actual travel. | None required initially; add purpose-specific actions only after the mechanism is designed. No humanoid retargeting or pet gesture. |
| Cometa | `Idle`, `Walk`, `Trot`, `TurnLeft`, `TurnRight`, `Stop`, `Sit`, `Wait`, `Recall`: four-paw locomotion, settled wait and a response to a recall order, with actual travel governed by the safe controller. | `Sniff` **only in pressurized areas**; `WagTail`, `ReceivePet`, `LookAtBeacon` for an already player-visible beacon, `WatchRepair` while a human repairs. No literal dog repair or talk action. |

The astronaut's jump clips must reflect actual controller takeoff, airborne and landing state, including interruption or pause; never drive height, jump timing or collision from animation. Human and canine Earth and lunar locomotion should be visibly distinct and grounded, with lunar cadence art-directed rather than claimed as exact physiology. Cometa's Earth/lunar `Walk` and `Trot` variants are authoring targets, as are corresponding transitions; orbit/cabin is a separate environment. Do not change existing player speeds, response or gravity incidentally.

Author human gait with left/right foot contact and release markers; landing and takeoff use actual ground-contact markers. Quadruped gait requires front-left, front-right, rear-left and rear-right paw contact/release and support-joint tracking; preserve valid support and grounded boots, restrain spine movement, and validate suit, pack and ear/helmet clearance at intermediate frames. Mechanical travel uses measured track/wheel phase or surface-contact markers appropriate to the actual mechanism, not fabricated human footfalls. Actions and cues may have visual markers but no markers confer resource effects. The manifest records authored marker times; their visual and runtime behavior still require review.

## Runtime integration design

1. Validate a local GLB and its approved manifest against actual imported nodes, skeletons, `AnimationGroup` names and clip bounds. Instantiate each actor with independent skeleton and animation state; shared immutable asset data is acceptable, shared mutable pose is not. Dispose each instance and groups with its region/actor lifetime. A missing/invalid clip falls back to a stable pose and a visible diagnostic, not an invisible or broken actor.
2. Make wrapper/controller the single authority for collision, position and yaw. Strip or compensate authored root translation and turns for gameplay while keeping the original source for preview. Apply gait speed/phase based on measured controller velocity and authored stride; do not double root motion or controller yaw. For turn interruptions match actual evaluated local joint transforms to sampled phases using anatomy-appropriate joints, including all four paws for Cometa; cache mappings, rebuild on rig/clip/convention change, preserve a closer existing phase. Compare intermediate poses, not just mixer weights. No fixed sample count or blend duration until tested.
3. Map controller and interaction states to each rig’s core locomotion, jump and context actions; support movement, direction reversal, new orders, pause, disable, region change and disposal during any transition. A contextual action can be interrupted immediately without changing simulation outcome. Visual contact events (footsteps, sound or dust) use verified clip markers and game clock, are cancelled on interruption and never mutate inventory or progression. Verify actual `AnimationGroup` event dispatch behavior before selecting the event implementation; do not assume imported events fire.
4. Integrate each actor instance independently. On pause freeze both animation evaluation and event clock; resume without replaying events. Reduced-motion settings should remove nonessential secondary/head motion while preserving readable contact and control. Explicitly test instance isolation, disposal and re-creation.
5. The current version 4 save envelope persists simulation version 3 companion state and supports earlier-envelope migrations. Validate saved target and navigation behavior after reload; persisted simulation state alone does not establish visually continuous clip phase. Pause/background/region changes must not silently run effects. A reload may deliberately restart an action in a stable pose if documented and avoids duplicated events.

## Production stages and provisional budgets

1. Audit current models and source rights; approve turnarounds and mechanical/anatomical feasibility.
2. Build editable rigs and bind poses; export neutral local GLBs and validate actual matrices, dimensions and independent instances.
3. Author and review required clips and variants; record real bounds, source frames, contacts, markers and root samples. Inspect isolated playback, gait seams, paw support, suit and helmet clearance.
4. Integrate behind a feature boundary with controller authority, transitions, event cancellation and save migration. Replace existing procedural pivot animation deliberately, never run it over the same targets concurrently.
5. Test in game camera on Earth/lunar routes and real browser GPU; collect captures and performance evidence before release.

Initial planning ceilings per character are **provisional, unmeasured**: 20k triangles, 64 deform joints, 3 materials and 2K texture set; lower-cost LOD and texture sharing are desirable. Review total visible characters, draw calls, memory and target-device frame time against actual baseline before setting final budgets. These ceilings are not measurements or promises; transparent helmet cost and multiple characters require profiling.

## Acceptance evidence — visual and device review pending

- Asset audit: source and local GLB provenance, valid material/skin, actual axes, hierarchy, skeleton and bind matrices, scene/world transforms, dimensions and clip bounds; no invented source frames.
- Clip approval: each core action and authored Earth/lunar variant visible at normal speed; extended clips validated when delivered; contact/release times from actual authored clips, loop continuity, measured in-place displacement/yaw; inspect weighted intermediate joint transforms, floor penetration/sliding, all four paws, ears/helmet and suit/pack clearance.
- Integration: actual `AnimationGroup` time, weight and target poses through walk/run/turn/stop and context interruptions; left/right, small/large/reversed turns, repeated orders, pause/resume, disable/re-enable, region transitions and save/reload with valid and missing companion targets. Confirm event cancellation, no doubled translation/yaw or gameplay/resource effect, independent poses for two instances and cleanup after repeated loads.
- Route/companion: ramps, obstacles, safe recall, optional absence, beacon visibility, no long jump requirement and no companion-dependent main path. Check accounting or explicitly documented aggregate life support, including reload and pause.
- Capture real browser GPU playback from game camera and isolated preview: device, browser/GPU, resolution, build/commit, settings, timing, active actors, frame time and captures. Record failures and blocked checks. NullEngine/unit tests do not prove visual or GPU acceptance.

Concept boards, including the lunar hero image, communicate target poses and mood; captions do not assert that depicted poses are exported clips or runtime assets.
