# Submission draft — ASTRA: Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer

**Draft for human review.** This independent prototype is not an official submission. Confirm the current challenge wording, project format, team registration, local timing and treatment of prior work with the organizers before using this text. No award eligibility or NASA endorsement is asserted.

## The idea

What happens when a cadet must make a lunar base work as a connected system? ASTRA turns that question into a small third-person exploration experience. At the fictional Aurora outpost, learners inspect equipment, make a choice, watch resource measurements change and use the result to plan their next action. Short narrative prompts and companions support exploration; the deterministic simulation determines resource outcomes.

## Four pillars in the prototype

1. **Energy:** repair generation equipment and compare power demand, battery reserves and the cost of interventions. The storm reduces modeled solar generation.
2. **Life support:** manage potable water, wastewater recycling and electrolysis; retrieve a finite ice sample and process it into water. These actions depend on functioning equipment and power.
3. **Radiation:** watch for a fictional storm warning, return to shelter and observe the consequences of exposure. The model is not a radiation dose calculation or an operational safety guide.
4. **Food:** plant and tend a crop that needs power, water and time; observe how its abstract harvest affects food reserves.

Training at the Earth academy, cargo and field-loadout choices, discovery in the canyon, Scout's repair and an optional companion support this loop. Cometa can follow or wait without awarding resources.

The intended learning cycle is **inspect → propose a hypothesis → intervene → observe evidence and resource trends → reflect and plan**. Hypotheses are prompts rather than graded scientific proof, and no study has established learning outcomes.

## Demonstration walkthrough

Start at the academy and inspect the training systems. Continue through the cabin, review cargo, and arrive at Aurora. Inspect and repair base equipment, observing the power and water readings. Choose field tools, reach Scout and the canyon discovery points, record a hypothesis and retrieve ice. Return, deliver the sample and watch processing and reserves. Monitor the storm warning, seek shelter, then inspect the greenhouse and mission evidence. Actual navigation time and accessibility depend on browser and device; consult `TEST_REPORT.md` for recorded results. This fictional sequence is not a NASA operational protocol.
