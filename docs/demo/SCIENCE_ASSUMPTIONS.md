# Demo science and simulation assumptions

This is an independent, disposable educational demonstration, not an official competition entry or a mission engineering model. Fictional regions and rates are chosen for legible play. One simulation tick is one second of play and represents 0.01 mission hours; advancing the clock faster also advances every resource and hazard. No wall clock or background progression is used.

The player observes, changes a system, and compares the resulting measurements. A recorded hypothesis is a prompt for investigation, not a graded answer or proof of learning. The cycle is inspect → intervene → observe resource trends → plan the next intervention. The academy gives a low-cost trial before the same balance matters at the habitat.

- Power uses kW and kWh. Per tick, generation and demand are multiplied by 0.01 h; unmet demand causes a blackout. Battery capacity is 12 kWh. Battery charging and discharging each have 90% efficiency; losses and surplus generation that cannot be stored are recorded separately. Storms reduce solar output to 45%. Repair and Scout charging draw from the battery; charge is not created by a companion.
- Water, oxygen, hydrogen, food and ice are in kg. Crew water use moves potable water to wastewater. Recycling returns 80% of processed wastewater to potable water; the remainder is recorded as recycler loss. Electrolysis consumes water and outputs oxygen and hydrogen in the ideal mass ratio 8:1, so generated mass equals water consumed. Consumables and crop irrigation are simplified; crop irrigation is recorded as water retained in the crop. The crop harvest is an abstract biological yield and is not a complete plant carbon or mass model.
- Ice is a finite 8 kg deposit. A 0.5 kg sample moves to carried inventory, then to delivered inventory; after powered processing it moves into potable water. The delivered and processed totals are cumulative ledger counters, not additional physical reservoirs.
- Repairs take five ticks and 0.2 kWh each. Scout costs one of two initial spare parts, 0.3 kWh and five ticks; its small repair-work load also appears in habitat demand. The simulated Scout battery charges from the main battery.
- Storms are a deterministic gameplay hazard: warning begins at lunar tick 180, providing 120 ticks to return to shelter; active exposure at 300–379, repeating every 600 ticks. These timings are not forecasts of real solar events, and sheltered status abstracts shielding and human radiation dose.
- The food crop needs 180 powered ticks with water; the yield and rates are gameplay approximations. The demo does not model full ecology, thermal control, pressure, gas storage, radiation dosimetry, or engineering safety margins. Reserves and timings are tuned for interaction rather than predictions of actual missions.

The save is version 2. State evolves in ordered one-second fixed ticks, preserving fractional remainder, and carries no external time. Failed runs need a previously saved checkpoint; restoring it restores the full state. Evidence is bounded to the most recent 100 records. No arbitrary action can award additional resources or repeated harvests.

A paced automated playthrough includes 60 ticks before starting repairs, 10 ticks between each five-tick repair, 20 ticks to the greenhouse, 15 to the field locker, 25 to Scout, 10 to the ice, 25 back, 10 to deliver and 15 to the shelter. Shelter is reached at lunar tick 230, 70 ticks before the first storm begins. After the storm and a 20-tick trip back to the greenhouse, the crop is ready and the mission completes with positive reserves. This demonstrates the specified sequence, not a measurement of real player navigation or a 20–30 minute playtest.

World target proximity is enforced by the presentation layer; the pure simulation checks phase, route, resources, and quest prerequisites but does not independently know the player's coordinates. Save validation checks version, shapes, numeric bounds, route consistency, and evidence formatting; it is not a cryptographic anti-tamper system.
